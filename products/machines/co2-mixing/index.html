Why over-carbonating costs money twice

Breweries often carbonate slightly high because the filler loses CO2 to foaming. That extra gas is bought twice: once as CO2 and once as lost yield at the filler. Holding ±0.05 vol removes the need for the buffer.

Inline measurement closes the loop

A continuous CO2 meter downstream of the dosing point feeds a PID controller. Without measurement, dosing is open-loop and drifts with product temperature and flow changes across a run.

Static mixer versus venturi

A static mixer gives better dissolution at lower line rates and lower pressure drop. A venturi injects more aggressively and suits high rates where the mixer would be very long. The choice follows line rate and available pump head.

Typical Applications

  • Breweries carbonating in-line before the filler — Inline carbonation removes a bright tank step and lets the filler receive beer already at target.
  • Lines with variable throughput — PID control holds accuracy when the line slows for a downstream stop, which is where open-loop systems drift.
  • Breweries carbonating soft drinks and water — The same unit handles carbonated water and soft drinks with a recipe change.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can it carbonate to 4 volumes?

Yes, provided product temperature is held near 0-2 degrees C. Above 3.5 vol, a longer saturation line is recommended.

Does it work with high-gravity dilution?

Yes. Deaerated water blending and CO2 dosing are usually combined in one skid for high-gravity brewing.

How often does the CO2 meter need calibration?

Every six months, against a reference method. Drift beyond 0.03 vol between calibrations is a sign the sensor needs service.
