Why include an HLT at this size

On a two-vessel 1,000 L system the kettle is also the hot liquor source, which means strike water for tomorrow's brew can only be heated after today's cast-out. A separate 1,000 L HLT removes that sequencing constraint and is what allows three brews a day instead of two.

Where the 36 kW goes

Electric 36 kW heats 1,000 L from 15 to 78 degrees C in roughly 100 minutes. If your tariff has a demand charge, steam is often cheaper at this size because the boiler can be sized to the average rather than the peak.

Typical Applications

  • Breweries planning around a 1,000 batch — At 2-3 per day the brewhouse delivers roughly 5,000 hl of finished beer per year on a five-day week, which sets the cellar and packaging requirement downstream.
  • Sites with 18 of available floor space — The 2 (mash/lauter tun + kettle/whirlpool) + 1,000 L HLT layout fits within 18 m2 including HLT, and the vessel arrangement determines how much of that space must stay clear for cleaning access.
  • Plants committed to electric heating — Electric 36 kW or steam 0.3 t/h is the practical choice here: it removes the boiler room below 2,000 L per batch, while larger sizes recover the cost of steam through faster ramp rates and tighter control.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can the HLT be added later?

Yes, but the frame and piping layout should be specified at order. Adding an HLT to a delivered two-vessel skid means re-piping the water manifold.

Is 1,000 L enough to start a taproom?

It covers roughly 500-1,200 hl per year at two to three brews a week, which is the typical taproom plus local wholesale range.

What is included with the 1,000 configuration?

The supply covers the 2 (mash/lauter tun + kettle/whirlpool) + 1,000 L HLT set, interconnecting sanitary piping, the control panel and CIP routing, with Electric 36 kW or steam 0.3 t/h as specified. Cellar tanks, packaging and building works are separate scope, so the plant can be sized to your own distribution plan rather than a fixed package.
