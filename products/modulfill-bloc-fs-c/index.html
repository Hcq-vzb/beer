Why one frame instead of three machines

Rinser, filler and crowner share a common frame and a single drive. Bottles transfer by neck-handling star wheels with no open conveyor between stations, which removes the main source of oxygen pickup and foaming on a conventional line. The shorter transfer also cuts floor space by roughly 30 percent compared with three separate machines.

Counter-pressure filling for dissolved oxygen control

Bottles are pre-evacuated and pre-pressurised with CO2 before the filling valve opens, so beer never meets air at atmospheric pressure. Short filling tubes keep contact time low and let the machine run high foam-free speeds even at 4 degrees C product temperature.

Format changeover

Neck handling means only the star wheels and the crowner chute change between bottle formats. A trained operator completes a 330 ml to 500 ml changeover in about 25 minutes without tools, which matters when a brewery runs several SKUs per shift.

Typical Applications

  • Craft and regional breweries with mixed SKUs — Best fit where the same line runs 330 ml, 500 ml and 750 ml bottles across a week and changeover time directly limits capacity.
  • Breweries protecting shelf life — Total package oxygen under 50 ppb supports 6-9 month best-before dates without flash pasteurisation.
  • Lines replacing three separate machines — A single bloc frees about 4 m of line length and one operator position compared with separate rinser, filler and crowner.

Frequently Asked Questions

What output should I specify?

Match rated output to your filler's peak hour plus 10 percent, not to annual volume. A 24,000 BPH frame typically serves 150,000-300,000 hl per year across two shifts.

Can it run crown and aluminium caps?

The standard crowner handles crown corks. An aluminium screw cap module is available as a bolt-on unit on the same frame.

Does it need a separate rinser?

No. The rinser is integrated and shares the drive, water recovery loop and CIP circuit with the filler.
